### 7c. Chrystal and Mizen -- quote CONFIRMED; "instead point to his companion paper" OVERSTATED

- 2003 publication: Crossref: "Goodhart's Law: its origins, meaning and implications for monetary policy", container "Central Banking, Monetary Theory and Practice", Edward Elgar Publishing, issued 2003-05-28, DOI 10.4337/9781781950777.00022. The text I read is the 12 November 2001 working-paper version ("Prepared for the Festschrift in honour of Charles Goodhart to be held on 15-16 November 2001 at the Bank of England"); the published 2003 text was not opened.
- Quote "(jocular) aside rather than the main point of the paper": source: "The context is very clear, but since the statement of the law was a (jocular) aside rather than the main point of the paper, the interpretation is open to some questions." CONFIRMED, character-exact.
- Which paper they point to. Verbatim: "The original statement of Goodhart's law can be found in one of two papers delivered by Charles to a conference in July 1975 at the Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA), Goodhart (1975a, b)1. It reached a wider audience when the key paper was published in a volume edited by Courakis (1981) and then again in a volume of Charles' own papers (Goodhart, 1984)." Then: "Goodhart (1975a) outlines how research originating from the Bank and that of outside academics had indicated that there was a stable money demand function in the UK. ... The relevant section of the key paper reads as follows:" [long quotation, attributed "(Goodhart, 1975)"]. Then: "This was clear well before 1975, but Goodhart (1975b) was a summary of the current problems of monetary management, as the title suggests." Reference list: "Goodhart, C.A.E. (1975a) 'Monetary Relationships: A View from Threadneedle Street' ... Goodhart, C.A.E. (1975a) 'Problems of Monetary Management: The UK Experience'" (both labelled 1975a) and "Goodhart, C.A.E. (1984) Monetary Theory and Practice, Macmillan: Basingstoke."
  So: by their letter labels, the law-bearing "key paper" (1975a) is "Monetary Relationships" and 1975b is "Problems"; but their reprint history says the key paper was reprinted in Goodhart (1984), whose chapter (per Springer TOC) is "Problems of Monetary Management" and which contains no "Monetary Relationships" chapter. C&M do not explicitly name the companion paper as the law's home; the draft's "instead point to his companion paper" reads their ambiguous labels as a definite attribution. Verdict: OVERSTATED. Proposed minimal fix: "Chrystal and Mizen, whose 2003 paper is the standard account, label the passage as coming from his companion paper in the same volume, though their own reprint history points back to *Problems of Monetary Management*, and they add that Goodhart offered the line as a "(jocular) aside rather than the main point of the paper."" This slightly softens the footnote's "genuinely unsettled" framing but keeps it.
- The law's wording on line 87, "any observed statistical regularity will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes": C&M present it in quotation marks as Goodhart's own text: "Goodhart's Law is the statement missing from the square brackets in the quotation above. It says: "Ignoring Goodhart's law, that any observed statistical regularity will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes"." So it is Goodhart's 1975 wording as transmitted by C&M, not C&M's paraphrase. The 1975/1984 primary text itself could not be opened (see 7a), so this rests on C&M's quotation. Line 87 presents it without quotation marks, which is appropriate.
